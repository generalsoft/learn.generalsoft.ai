Meaning, Use, and Understanding

Four short experiments connecting philosophy of language to what actually happens inside a language model: representation as geometry, meaning as use, speech acts as pragmatics, and autonomy as a governed loop.

Representation & Vector Geometry

Frege distinguished a word's sense (how it is presented) from its reference (what it points to). A model has no reference at all — only position in a learned geometric space. Type the same ambiguous word into two different sentences and watch its coordinates move.

Speech Acts & Pragmatics

Searle argued that an utterance performs several acts at once: what it literally says, what it's actually doing, and what it brings about in the hearer. Click a case to pull it apart.

The intentionality row reflects Searle's felicity conditions: the propositional content, the preparatory conditions that must hold, the sincerity condition (the speaker's actual mental state), and the essential condition (what the utterance counts as). A language model can reproduce the surface form of any of these acts without satisfying the sincerity condition — it has no belief that it's cold, no intention to call tomorrow.

Agentic Harness: Governance & Sandboxing

An agent loop (Thought → Action → Observation, repeated) is only as safe as the harness around it. Run three scenarios and watch where the guardrails intervene.

status: idle iterations: 0 circuit breaker: armed

This is a scripted simulation of a ReAct-style loop, not a live model call. The human-in-the-loop gate blocks execution of any action tagged as irreversible or destructive until a person approves it. The circuit breaker halts the loop after a fixed number of iterations without a terminating observation, regardless of how confident the agent's own reasoning sounds — the harness doesn't trust the model to know when it's stuck.
